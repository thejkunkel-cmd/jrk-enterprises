# Layer 6: Evaluation
## 01_eight-layer-framework | proJeKt: humAnIty

**Origin:** Esra Kucukciftci (AI Outcome Economics) + JRK Enterprises
**Domain:** Economic / Human
**Position in Stack:** Layer 6 of 8 — First Human-Centered Layer

> "You cannot optimize what you cannot evaluate. And you cannot evaluate what you cannot measure by human outcomes."

---

## Definition

The Evaluation layer is the first layer that does not exist in Jensen Huang's original five-layer technical stack. It is the bridge between technical deployment and human accountability. This layer encompasses:

- Independent assessment of AI systems across all five technical layers
- Measurement of AI outcomes against human-defined success criteria
- Institutional adoption evaluation frameworks
- Competitive analysis of AI tools (e.g., Microsoft vs. Perplexity evaluation)
- Client-facing AI readiness assessments
- Resonance scoring (see 07_resonance-logs)

---

## Theoretical Foundation

This layer draws from **Esra Kucukciftci's AI Outcome Economics** framework, which argues that the economic value of AI must be measured not by technical performance metrics but by actual human and institutional outcomes.

Key principles from this framework:
- AI ROI must be calculated in terms of human capacity gained, not just task completion
- Evaluation must be independent of the vendors being evaluated
- Institutional adoption without evaluation is governance failure

---

## Role in the Eight-Layer Economy

Layer 6 creates the accountability surface for the entire stack. Without evaluation:
- Organizations adopt AI based on marketing rather than evidence
- Harm caused by AI systems goes unmeasured and unaddressed
- The economic value of AI accrues only to vendors, not to human users

---

## JRK Enterprises Application

The 02_evaluation folder contains the active evaluation framework, including:
- Layer-by-layer scoring rubrics
- Microsoft vs. Perplexity comparative analysis
- Client AI readiness assessment templates
- Institutional adoption evaluation guides

---

## Human Impact Considerations (Safety Spine Application)

- Human evaluators must review AI-scored human work (per Safety Spine)
- Evaluation independence must be maintained — no vendor may evaluate their own products
- Evaluation results must be transparent and challengeable
- Communities affected by AI must have voice in evaluation criteria
