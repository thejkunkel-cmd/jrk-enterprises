## Definition

The Data layer encompasses the collection, curation, storage, and governance of data that trains and powers AI systems. This includes:

- Training data collection and labeling
- Data pipelines and ETL processes
- Vector databases and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)
- Data governance and compliance frameworks
- Synthetic data generation
- Real-time data integration

---

## Role in the Eight-Layer Economy

Data is the raw material of AI. The quality, provenance, and ethics of data determine the quality, reliability, and ethics of the model. Key economic factors:

- **Data moats**: Proprietary datasets create competitive advantages
- **Data labor**: Human labelers and annotators often work in precarious conditions
- **Data sovereignty**: Who owns the data used to train models that affect communities
- **Data freshness**: Real-time vs. static data determines system recency

---

## Human Impact Considerations (Safety Spine Application)

- Consent and provenance required for all training data (per Safety Spine)
- Data labor ethics: annotators must be fairly compensated and protected
- Community data sovereignty: data about communities should benefit those communities
- Personal data: no personal data may be used without explicit consent
- The unnamed credit system in PROVENANCE.md extends to data contributors

---

## Evaluation Criteria (Layer 6 Connection)

- Data provenance documentation
- Consent and licensing audit
- Bias representation in training data
- Data labeler compensation and working conditions
- Privacy compliance (GDPR, CCPA, etc.)
