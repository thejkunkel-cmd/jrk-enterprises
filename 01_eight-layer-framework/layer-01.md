## Definition

The Infrastructure layer encompasses all physical and cloud-based computing resources required to train, deploy, and run AI models at scale. This includes:

- GPU and specialized AI accelerator hardware (NVIDIA H100, A100, etc.)
- Data center infrastructure and power systems
- Cloud computing platforms (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud, CoreWeave, etc.)
- Networking infrastructure enabling high-bandwidth model communication
- Cooling and physical plant systems

---

## Role in the Eight-Layer Economy

Layer 1 is the foundation on which all other layers depend. No model can run without infrastructure. No application can function without a model. The infrastructure layer determines:

- **Cost**: Compute costs flow through every product and service built on AI
- **Access**: Who has access to infrastructure determines who has access to AI capability
- **Scale**: Infrastructure limits constrain the size and speed of AI systems
- **Geography**: Physical infrastructure location affects latency, regulation, and sovereignty

---

## Human Impact Considerations (Safety Spine Application)

- Infrastructure concentration creates power asymmetries (few companies control most compute)
- Energy consumption of AI infrastructure has significant environmental implications
- Audit trails must be maintained for all model deployments (per Safety Spine Rule 1)
- Access equity is a Layer 1 concern: who can afford to use infrastructure determines who benefits from AI

---

## Evaluation Criteria (Layer 6 Connection)

When evaluating an AI system at Layer 1, assessors should consider:

- Reliability and uptime guarantees
- Geographic distribution and latency
- Environmental impact (PUE, renewable energy %)
- Access pricing and equity
- Audit and logging capabilities
