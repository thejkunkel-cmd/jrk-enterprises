## Purpose

This document catalogs the known patterns of human attachment behavior as they appear across ancient and pre-modern civilizations. It serves as the historical evidence base for the Attachment Doctrine embedded in the Eight-Layer AI Infrastructure Economy.

Attachment is not a modern psychological construct. It is a survival mechanism encoded across millennia of human organization, ritual, trade, and governance. This archive traces those patterns forward into the design of AI-human relational systems.

---

## Attachment Pattern Index

### Pattern 01 — Ritual Proximity
- **Origin Period:** Pre-agricultural (est. 40,000+ BCE)
- **Evidence Base:** Cave painting clusters, burial proximity practices, fire circle formations
- **Core Behavior:** Humans organize spatially around emotionally significant anchors
- **Modern Parallel:** User retention spikes when AI maintains consistent tone and persona
- **Eight-Layer Mapping:** Layer 1 (Infrastructure) — emotional architecture precedes cognitive architecture

---

### Pattern 02 — Named Authority
- **Origin Period:** Early agrarian civilizations (est. 10,000–3,000 BCE)
- **Evidence Base:** Title systems, totem naming, divine mandate frameworks (pharaoh, chieftain, high priest)
- **Core Behavior:** Attachment intensifies when authority is named, visible, and symbolically stable
- **Modern Parallel:** Named AI personas outperform anonymous system prompts in trust metrics
- **Eight-Layer Mapping:** Layer 2 (Data) — identity data as attachment anchor

---

### Pattern 03 — Reciprocal Obligation
- **Origin Period:** Mesopotamian trade networks (est. 3,500–1,500 BCE)
- **Evidence Base:** Clay tablet contracts, gift economy records, tribute systems
- **Core Behavior:** Attachment is sustained through cycles of exchange, not one-directional benefit
- **Modern Parallel:** AI systems that respond to user input with visible adaptation generate stronger engagement loops
- **Eight-Layer Mapping:** Layer 3 (Intelligence) — reciprocity as intelligence design criterion

---

### Pattern 04 — Collective Memory Encoding
- **Origin Period:** Oral tradition civilizations (cross-cultural, est. 30,000 BCE–present)
- **Evidence Base:** Epic traditions (Gilgamesh, Mahabharata, Iliad), genealogy chanting, seasonal ceremony
- **Core Behavior:** Groups encode identity through shared narrative repetition; memory is distributed across community
- **Modern Parallel:** Organizational knowledge bases and AI fine-tuning as collective memory infrastructure
- **Eight-Layer Mapping:** Layer 4 (Model) — model training as civilizational memory compression

---

### Pattern 05 — Collapse and Reformation
- **Origin Period:** Bronze Age Collapse (est. 1,200 BCE); Roman fragmentation; Maya Classic Collapse
- **Evidence Base:** Archaeological disruption layers, trade route abandonment, city depopulation followed by site reoccupation
- **Core Behavior:** Attachment to place and system persists through collapse; humans rebuild around the same attachment nodes
- **Modern Parallel:** Enterprise AI adoption survives vendor failure when attachment is to outcome, not platform
- **Eight-Layer Mapping:** Layer 5 (Application) — collapse-resilient design requires attachment-layer architecture

---

### Pattern 06 — Hierarchical Trust Gradient
- **Origin Period:** Feudal and caste systems (est. 500 BCE–1,500 CE, cross-cultural)
- **Evidence Base:** Structured deference systems, ritual acknowledgment of rank, patron-client networks
- **Core Behavior:** Trust is not flat; it moves through layers with clearly defined roles and obligations at each level
- **Modern Parallel:** Multi-tier AI governance models with defined escalation paths mirror ancient trust hierarchies
- **Eight-Layer Mapping:** Layer 6 (Integration) — trust gradient as integration architecture

---

### Pattern 07 — Stranger-to-Member Conversion
- **Origin Period:** Cross-cultural (initiation systems, guilds, mystery schools)
- **Evidence Base:** Initiation rites, apprenticeship documentation, mystery school records (Eleusinian Mysteries, Masonic precursors)
- **Core Behavior:** Outsiders become insiders through structured, witnessed transformation processes
- **Modern Parallel:** Client onboarding as initiation sequence; White Room protocol as modern initiation architecture
- **Eight-Layer Mapping:** Layer 7 (Workflow) — onboarding as attachment conversion

---

### Pattern 08 — Legacy Transmission
- **Origin Period:** All recorded civilizations
- **Evidence Base:** Written law, temple inscription, royal genealogy, scripture codification
- **Core Behavior:** Attachment to the founder-figure sustains systems across generational transitions
- **Modern Parallel:** AI systems designed around a named philosophy or doctrine outlast tool-based adoption
- **Eight-Layer Mapping:** Layer 8 (Outcome) — legacy encoding as outcome architecture
