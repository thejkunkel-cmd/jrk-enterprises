## Executive Summary

Every bubble in recorded financial history — from Dutch tulip mania in 1637 to the dot-com crash of 2000 to the housing collapse of 2008 — was obvious in hindsight and invisible in the present, because the underlying technology or asset was *genuinely transformative*. The internet was real. Housing democratized wealth, briefly. AI is changing everything. The question has never been whether the technology is real. It is whether the **valuation of the infrastructure surrounding that technology has detached from any tether to recoverable value**.

Masayoshi Son calls the AI revolution "50 times bigger than the dotcom boom." If the historical correction ratio holds even partially, the arithmetic of a downturn is staggering. The dotcom bust wiped out more than $5 trillion in market capitalization. An AI correction of equivalent proportion would be historically unprecedented. This document is not a prediction of when that happens. It is a **watch system and preparation protocol** — the architecture that ensures the Center holds when the ground shifts beneath everything built on top of AI infrastructure.

## Part One: The Universal Anatomy of a Bubble

### Five Common Features Across 400 Years

Across nine major documented bubbles spanning four centuries, financial historians have identified five common structural features:

1. **A belief in a "new era" or transformative technology.** The technology is always genuinely real. The problem is not the technology — it is the assumption that *first-mover ownership of the infrastructure* equals permanent, recoverable returns.

2. **Deregulation and financial innovation.** In AI, the financial innovation is the wholesale extension of depreciation schedules — turning a 3-year hardware cycle into a 6-year paper asset — to inflate earnings and justify valuations.

3. **Easy and abundant credit.** Amazon, Microsoft, Alphabet, and Meta combined spent $52.8 billion in capex in a single quarter (Q2 2024), up 60% year-over-year.

4. **Justification of new valuation metrics.** Today the equivalent is the normalization of AI infrastructure spending as a non-negotiable "strategic advantage," with CEOs explicitly signaling they will invest "whether you like it or not."

5. **Accounting problems and post-bubble scandals.** Michael Burry has flagged the systematic extension of server depreciation life cycles by major hyperscalers — a practice he estimates could inflate Big Tech earnings by up to $176 billion between 2026 and 2028.
