## The Unnamed Credit System

**Default:** Contributors are credited by name and professional role
where their identity is publicly known and clearly attributable.

**When direct linking is not possible** (LinkedIn access restrictions,
link rot, connection-gated content): the person is credited by name
and role without the unstable URL, with a note explaining why no
direct link is provided. LinkedIn's access restrictions do not reduce
our attribution obligation — they increase our documentation burden.

**The Claim Process:** If you believe your publicly posted work is
reflected in this project and you wish to be formally acknowledged
or to dispute how your work is represented:

1. Identify yourself and the specific work you believe is reflected
2. Describe how and where you believe it appears in this project
3. Submit your claim via the contact path at [project contact — TBD]
4. Your claim will be reviewed against the source log below
5. If verified, your credit entry will be updated and you will be
   notified of any changes

This process exists because the system believes that public intellectual
contribution deserves acknowledgment — and that the right response to
AI-assisted IP development is more transparency, not less.

## LinkedIn Source Handling Protocol

When a LinkedIn post is used as a source in this project:

1. **CAPTURE** at point of discovery: screenshot + manual text
   transcription where possible
2. **ATTRIBUTE**: Name, title, company, post date, approximate URL
   if accessible and stable
3. **LOG**: Add entry to this document with credit type noted
4. **UNNAMED DEFAULT**: If the post cannot be directly linked due to
   LinkedIn access restrictions, the person is credited by name and
   role. The platform's restrictions are noted explicitly.
5. **CLAIM PATH**: Any person who believes their work appears here
   may submit a claim via the guided verification process above.

**Policy statement:** LinkedIn's API restrictions and access controls
are a platform business decision. They do not transfer citation
responsibility away from this project. Where LinkedIn makes proper
attribution technically harder, this project documents more carefully
— not less.
