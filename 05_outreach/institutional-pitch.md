## Section 1: The Problem

### AI deployment is outpacing human readiness.

Organizations across every sector are integrating AI tools into operations, workflows, and decision-making — often without any structured framework for how humans are supposed to work alongside them.

This is not a technology problem. The technology exists. The gap is human infrastructure:

- How do people learn to trust AI appropriately — not blindly, not dismissively?
- How do organizations govern AI behavior without understanding the attachment dynamics underneath it?
- How do operators avoid burnout, boundary collapse, and cognitive dependency?
- Who is responsible when AI influences a consequential decision and no one logged why?

These are not future questions. They are happening now, at every level of every organization deploying AI today.

### The window for getting this right is narrow.

AI adoption is not slowing. The organizations and individuals who build real working relationships with AI — grounded in governance, ethics, and human understanding — will have a structural advantage over those who simply bolted tools onto existing workflows and hoped for the best.

The infrastructure for that advantage is not coming from the AI labs. It is not coming from enterprise software vendors. It is coming from practitioners who understand both the technology and the human beings working with it.
